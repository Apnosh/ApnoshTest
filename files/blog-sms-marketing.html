SMS marketing: why text beats email for local business promotions

Email open rates hover around 20%. SMS open rates average 98% — read within 90 seconds. For time-sensitive local promotions, there's no comparison. Here's how to build a text list that drives real walk-ins.

The channel most local businesses ignore

Walk into any local restaurant, salon, or retailer and ask how they communicate with their customer list. Most will say email — or nothing at all. SMS marketing remains the most underused tool in local business marketing, mainly because owners assume it's expensive or complicated to set up.

It's neither. A basic SMS tool costs $20–$50/month. Building a list of 200–300 opted-in customers takes about 60 days. And one well-timed text on a slow Tuesday can fill a restaurant that would have had empty tables. No other channel comes close to that kind of immediate, measurable impact.

The takeaway: SMS wins on urgency and attention. Email wins on depth and cost per send. Run both — they serve different moments in the customer relationship.

The 4 SMS campaign types that work for local business

Campaign Type 1
Flash sale / limited offer
The highest-converting SMS type. Create urgency with a real deadline — same day or next 24 hours. Works best for restaurants (slow night fill), retail (end-of-season clearance), and services (open appointment slots).
🕐 Send 11am–2pm on slow days
[Business]: Flash deal — today only. Buy one entrée, get the second 50% off. Valid until 8pm tonight. Show this text. Reply STOP to opt out.
Avg. 12% conversion · ~18% redemption rate
Campaign Type 2
Event reminder
Sent 24 hours before an event, class, or special. Higher show-up rate than email reminders. Include the date, time, and a one-tap link to get directions.
🕐 Send 24h before the event
[Business]: Reminder — Live music + happy hour starts tomorrow at 6pm. Half-price apps until 7. We'd love to see you. [Directions link] · Reply STOP to opt out
Avg. 31% open-to-attend rate
Campaign Type 3
Loyalty reward
Reward your list for being subscribers. A "VIP text list" perk makes people stay opted in — and makes signing up feel worth it. Keep this to once a month so it stays special.
🕐 Send 1x per month, Friday morning
[Business]: VIP list perk — this weekend only. Show this text for a free upgrade on any order over $20. Our way of saying thanks. Valid Sat–Sun. · Reply STOP to opt out
Avg. 22% redemption · drives loyalty
Campaign Type 4
Win-back
For customers who haven't visited in 60+ days. The goal is one visit — not a long-term campaign. Keep it personal and simple. A small "welcome back" gesture is usually enough.
🕐 Trigger after 60 days of no visit
[Business]: Hi! We miss you. It's been a while — here's 15% off your next visit. No expiry, no catch. Just come see us soon. · Reply STOP to opt out
Avg. 9% reactivation rate

How to build your SMS list from zero

You need opt-in consent before texting anyone. Here are the fastest ways to build a compliant list:

Keyword opt-in: Put a sign at your counter — "Text DEALS to 55555 to get VIP specials." This is the fastest-growing method for in-store businesses. The customer texts a keyword, the platform auto-enrolls them and sends a welcome message.

Website widget: A small pop-up or sticky bar on your website with "Get exclusive deals by text." Converts best when the offer is specific ("text us for 10% off your first visit").

POS integration: When you capture a phone number at checkout — for loyalty programs, receipts, or reservations — ask for SMS opt-in consent at the same time. "Can we text you deals and updates? We only send 2–3 per month."

Social bio link: Add an SMS opt-in link to your Instagram and Facebook bios. "Join our VIP text list →" with a Linktree or landing page that collects number + consent.

Compliance: what you legally must do

SMS marketing in the US is governed by TCPA (Telephone Consumer Protection Act). Violations can cost $500–$1,500 per text. These rules aren't complicated — just follow them from day one:

⚖️ TCPA compliance checklist
Written opt-in consent required — "I agree to receive marketing texts from [Business]" must be checked or signed before you send anything.
Identify yourself in every text — Start each message with your business name. "[Business]:" is enough.
Include opt-out instructions — "Reply STOP to opt out" must appear in every message (most SMS platforms auto-append this).
Honor opt-outs immediately — When someone replies STOP, they must never receive another text from you. All compliant platforms handle this automatically.
Time restrictions — Only send texts between 8am–9pm in the recipient's local time zone.
Never use imported lists — You cannot buy a phone number list and text it. Every number must have explicit opt-in from that specific person for your specific business.
