Why 73% of local searches end at the top 3 results — and how to get there

The Google Maps top 3 isn’t just good visibility. It’s the only visibility that matters. Here’s the exact ranking formula we use across 200+ clients — and the changes that produce results in 30 days or less.

Search for any local business — "coffee shop near me," "best pizza in Austin," "plumber open now" — and Google shows you a map with three business pins. Those three spots get 73% of all clicks. Position four gets about 4%. Positions five through ten split the rest.

If you’re not in the top 3, you are effectively invisible to the majority of local searchers. This isn’t a guideline or an opinion — it’s a pattern we’ve measured across hundreds of campaigns.

How Google decides who ranks in the top 3

Google uses three signals to determine local map rankings. Understanding these is the entire game.

1. Relevance — do you match what they searched for?

Google matches search queries to business categories, services, and keywords in your Google Business Profile (GBP). If you’re a Korean BBQ restaurant but your GBP only says "restaurant," you are competing against every restaurant in your city. If it says "Korean BBQ, KBBQ, Korean barbecue, all-you-can-eat Korean," you surface for exactly the people looking for you.

2. Distance — how close are you to the searcher?

This one you can’t control directly. Google favors proximity. What you can do: make sure your address in your GBP matches your address on your website, on every directory, and on every citation. Discrepancies confuse Google’s location algorithm.

3. Prominence — do other sources trust you?

Prominence is the most actionable signal and the one most businesses neglect. It comes from three places: your review count and rating, links to your website from other sites, and consistency across the web (what SEOs call "citations").

The short version: Relevance + Distance + Prominence. You control two of the three. Focus there.

The 8 changes that move the needle fastest

After working with 200+ local businesses, we’ve identified the optimizations that produce the highest-impact results in the shortest time.

1
Claim and fully complete your Google Business Profile
Google says fully completed profiles get 7x more clicks than incomplete ones. Every field matters: hours, photos, services, description, attributes. If any field is blank, you’re leaving ranking on the table.
2
Choose the right primary category — and add secondary categories
Your primary category is the single most important relevance signal. Be specific. "Pizza restaurant" beats "restaurant." Then add relevant secondary categories for each service you offer.
3
Write a description that uses your actual search terms
Your GBP business description has 750 characters. Use them. Naturally mention your city, neighborhood, key services, and how you differ from competitors. Don’t keyword-stuff — write for a human first.
4
Get your NAP consistent across the web
Name, Address, Phone (NAP) must be identical everywhere: your website, Yelp, Facebook, Apple Maps, Bing, TripAdvisor, and every industry directory. One inconsistency can undermine your ranking. Run a citation audit quarterly.
5
Build your review volume and recency
Google values both the number of reviews and how recent they are. A business with 200 reviews from 3 years ago often loses to a business with 40 reviews from the last 6 months. You need a system that generates reviews consistently, not one big push.
6
Respond to every review — especially the negative ones
Google treats review responses as an engagement signal. Responding to negative reviews professionally also shows potential customers (and Google) that you’re an active, attentive business.
7
Post to your GBP weekly
GBP posts are like social media posts for your Google listing. They signal activity and freshness. Post updates, offers, events, and new products consistently. Businesses that post weekly outrank comparable businesses that don’t.
8
Add high-quality photos — and keep adding them
Listings with 100+ photos get 520% more calls than listings with no photos. That said, quality matters too. Professional photos of your space, food, products, and team outperform blurry phone snaps.

What to expect: a realistic timeline

Local SEO is not instant. Here’s what a typical timeline looks like when you implement these changes systematically:

  • Days 1–7: Complete your GBP, fix NAP, add photos. These are free and take a few hours.
  • Weeks 2–4: First review requests go out. You start seeing movement in "near me" searches where you were previously off the map.
  • Month 2: Consistent posting and new reviews start accumulating. Positions begin to shift, especially for long-tail searches.
  • Month 3: Most businesses that execute consistently hit the top 5 for their primary keywords.
  • Month 4–6: Top 3 is achievable in most markets with consistent execution. Highly competitive markets (multiple locations, franchise competition) take longer.

The one thing most businesses get wrong

They do a big optimization sprint and then stop. They clean up their GBP, get 20 reviews in one month, post for a few weeks, and then drop everything when they get busy.

Google rewards consistency. A business that posts once a week for six months outperforms one that posts ten times in January and disappears. A business that gets two or three new reviews every week outperforms one that gets 30 in a single month.

The businesses that hold top 3 spots long-term treat local SEO like a utility — something that runs in the background, consistently, month after month. The businesses that lose those spots are the ones that treat it like a project with an end date.
