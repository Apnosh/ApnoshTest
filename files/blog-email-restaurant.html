How one restaurant owner added $4,200/mo with a 4-email sequence

A simple post-visit follow-up turned one-time diners into regulars. Here’s the exact email flow they used — subject lines, timing, and full results included.

✉️
Apnosh TeamEmail Marketing
·June 28, 2026·4 min read
✉️

The owner of a casual Mexican restaurant in Austin came to us with a common problem: plenty of first-time visitors, not enough regulars. Their Google reviews were strong (4.6 stars, 140+ reviews), their food was good, but most people only came back two or three times a year — if at all.

The fix wasn’t a new marketing channel. It was a simple email sequence triggered every time a customer gave their email at checkout. Four emails. Sent automatically. Zero ongoing effort after setup.

Here are the results after 90 days:

$4,200
monthly revenue added from repeat visits
34%
open rate (industry avg: 21%)
2.1×
increase in 60-day return visit rate

The 4-email sequence (with exact subject lines)

Email 1Welcome
Sent: immediately after signup
Subject line
"Thanks for coming in — here's something for next time"
Short, warm, personal. Acknowledges the visit, thanks them by name, offers a simple incentive (10% off next visit, or a free chips and guac). No fluff. Just: glad you came, here’s a reason to return.

Key: this is not a promotional email. It reads like a message from the owner, not a marketing department.
CTA: "Book a table" or "Show this email next time you visit"
Email 2Story
Sent: 5 days later
Subject line
"Why we make our tortillas by hand (every single day)"
A behind-the-scenes story about how the food is made or where it comes from. In this case: the owner’s grandmother’s tortilla recipe, why they use a specific masa, why they bother when pre-made would be cheaper.

This email builds the relationship. It gives people a story to tell when they bring friends. It reminds them why this place is different from the chain down the street.
CTA: Soft nudge to check out a new menu item or upcoming event
Email 3Social Proof
Sent: 12 days after signup
Subject line
"What people say about the enchiladas (we blush every time)"
A curated set of 3–4 real Google reviews, focused on specific dishes or experiences. Pair each review with a photo of the dish. Let the customers do the talking.

For people who haven’t returned yet, this is a reminder of what they liked. For people who are on the fence about what to order next time, it’s a decision-maker.
CTA: "Leave us a review" (recapture Google review opportunity) + "See the full menu"
Email 4Re-engagement
Sent: 21 days after signup
Subject line
"Haven't seen you in a while — here's a reason to come back"
A simple, direct re-engagement email. Acknowledges the time that’s passed without making it weird. Offers a slightly stronger incentive than email 1 (free dessert, 15% off, buy-one-get-one appetizer).

This is the email that converts fence-sitters. The timing (3 weeks) is intentional — long enough that it doesn’t feel pushy, short enough that they still remember you.
CTA: Discount code or show-this-email offer with expiry date

Why this works (and why most email doesn’t)

The sequence above feels like a relationship, not a drip campaign. Each email has a reason to exist beyond "we want you to spend money." Email 1 thanks them. Email 2 tells a story. Email 3 lets customers speak. Email 4 gives a genuine reason to return.

Compare that to the typical restaurant email: a weekly "TACO TUESDAY 15% OFF" blast to a list they haven’t cleaned in two years. Those get 10% open rates and train customers to ignore you.

The list-building piece: None of this works without an email list. The restaurant collected emails at checkout with a simple iPad sign-up: "Get our menu updates and exclusive offers." Within 90 days they had 680 subscribers from real visitors. Our Email Marketing Starter Kit covers list-building strategies in detail — free download.

How to adapt this for your business

The four-email structure works for almost any local business where repeat customers are valuable. The specific content changes — a salon uses a "care tips for your new cut" email instead of a recipe story — but the logic is identical.

  • Email 1: Thank you + small incentive to return
  • Email 2: A story that builds connection and trust
  • Email 3: Social proof (reviews, testimonials, before/afters)
  • Email 4: Re-engagement offer with a real deadline

Set this up once. It runs automatically for every new customer who signs up, forever.
