The 5 marketing levers every local business should pull first

Not all marketing moves the needle equally. Here’s the priority order based on ROI data across 200+ local businesses — ranked from highest return to lowest, with what to do first this week.

📈
Apnosh TeamGrowth Strategy
·June 18, 2026·8 min read
📈

Every week, someone asks us: "Where should I start with marketing?" They’ve read about TikTok, heard about SEO, been pitched by three agencies, and now they’re paralyzed. The answer is almost always the same: ignore most of it and pull these five levers first, in this order.

This ranking comes from measuring revenue impact across 200+ local business marketing programs. These aren’t opinions — they’re patterns observed across restaurants, salons, gyms, contractors, retail shops, and service businesses in dozens of markets.

1
Your Google Business Profile (local SEO)
Highest ROI

73% of local search clicks go to the top 3 Google Maps results. If you’re not there, you’re invisible to the majority of people looking for exactly what you do, right now, in your area. These are the highest-intent buyers in marketing — they’re actively searching, not browsing.

Why it ranks first: No ad spend required. Your GBP is free. Fully optimizing it — complete info, real photos, active review strategy, weekly posts — produces measurable results within 60–90 days. We have clients who doubled their inbound call volume from this single lever.

First action this week: Log into your GBP, check every field for completeness, and make sure your hours are accurate. Then identify the last time you asked a customer for a review.

2
Email list and basic automation
$42 ROI per $1

Email delivers $42 in revenue for every $1 spent — the highest ROI of any marketing channel. For local businesses with existing customers, it’s usually the fastest path to incremental revenue because you’re reaching people who already chose you once.

Why it ranks second: It builds an asset you own. Your social following can disappear. Your email list can’t be taken from you. A simple 4-email post-visit sequence (welcome, story, social proof, re-engagement) consistently produces 30–50% higher repeat visit rates.

First action this week: Set up a simple email signup at your point of sale. An iPad with a "join our list for exclusive offers" prompt collects 5–15 new subscribers per day for most businesses.

3
Your website (conversion-optimized)
24/7 sales tool

The average local business website converts less than 1% of visitors. That means 99 people land on your site and leave without contacting you, booking, or buying. Fixing this doesn’t require a full redesign — it usually requires five targeted changes to what’s already there.

Why it ranks third: Once optimized, your website works for you 24 hours a day. Every other marketing effort — SEO, social, email — eventually drives traffic back to your website. A leaky website wastes all that effort.

First action this week: On your homepage, find your primary call-to-action (book, call, order, get a quote). Is it visible without scrolling? Is it specific? Does clicking it actually work on mobile? Fix those three things first.

4
Social media (with the right content mix)
Brand + trust

Social media ranks fourth — not because it doesn’t matter, but because its primary role for local businesses is trust-building and brand familiarity, not direct revenue. The customers who convert from social are usually ones who saw your GBP, checked your Instagram, and then called.

Why it ranks fourth: The ROI is indirect and longer-term than levers 1–3. But it matters for credibility. A business with no recent social posts raises questions. Three posts a week (before/after, behind-the-scenes, reviews) is enough for most local businesses.

First action this week: Post one before/after or customer testimonial today. Don’t overthink it. A real photo with real context outperforms produced content that took three days.

5
Paid advertising (once 1–4 are solid)
Amplifier

Paid ads — Google Search ads, Meta ads, Nextdoor — rank fifth not because they don’t work, but because they amplify what’s already there. Running ads to a broken website or a GBP with 8 reviews and blurry photos is burning money.

Why it ranks fifth: Ads are a multiplier. If levers 1–4 are working, ads pour fuel on what’s already burning. If they’re not, ads will show you exactly what’s broken — expensively.

First action this week: Before running any ads, audit where people land (your website or GBP) and what they see when they get there. Ads can wait until that experience is solid.

The order matters

We see businesses skip levers 1–3 and go straight to lever 5 because ads feel actionable and measurable. They are. But they’re also the most expensive way to discover that your website converts at 0.4% and your GBP hasn’t been updated since 2023.

The businesses that grow consistently almost always have a strong lever 1 (local SEO and reviews), a list they own (lever 2), and a website that doesn’t leak (lever 3) before they invest in amplification. That foundation is what makes everything else work.
